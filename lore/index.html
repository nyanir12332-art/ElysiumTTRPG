Lore

Overview

The City of Elysium is an urban mega structure the size of a small country, it has a population of around 7 billion spread apart by 14 layers and 26 districts. The City sits above The Ruins which holds it up above The Wastes.

Districts

The City is divided into twenty-six Districts, quite diverse in culture and aesthetics, each of which is governed by a Wing, a corporation that has ascended to claim complete control of said area. All Wings respond to the Head, the supreme governing power of the City. The arrangement of the Districts seems to be static, as there has been no mentioned case of a District expanding into a fallen Wing's territory. Each District is split into a Nest, a territory directly governed and protected by its Wing, and the Backstreets, expansive spaces of wilderness or slums, largely neglected by the Wings, where the Syndicates of the City run free.

The Head

The Head is the leader of the Wings and the central governing body of the City. It is in direct control of A Corp., B Corp., and C Corp., the three Districts at the peak of the City. The Head's identity is completely unknown, although it appears the term refers to a group of people rather than a singular entity; any detail regarding it is a closely guarded secret. The Head, through A Corp., retains control of the entire City. It is responsible for authorizing Wings, managing patents, and the minting of dollars, the currency used in the City. Despite this, it does not typically interfere directly with the affairs of the various Wings in their own Districts; rather, the Head's greatest involvement in the life of the City consists in establishing and enforcing the rules that relate to the overall ethics of the City.

The Head's will shapes the image of the City, as it wields the ability to decide when a part of it no longer fits in the vision, and to chase it outside its confines. Some speculate the Head's decisions in these matters aren't aimed at maintaining order, but rather that the Head wishes to preserve its ideal image of the City, and that for this reason they allow the various Stars of the City to keep existing. This is corroborated by the agents of the Head themselves referring to their work as cultivating the ecology of the City. To preserve this vision, the Head retains the unique power to assign the highest risk level in the scale of hazards of the City, that of Impuritas Civitatis, or Impurity of the City. The risk level of Impurity signifies a hazard that violates the ethics of the City, and that must be either destroyed or physically expelled from the confines of the City expeditiously.

The Head introduced the Artificial Creature Ethics Amendment, which established City-wide Taboos against creating artificial creatures with autonomous, humanlike intelligence, including both mechanical copies of a human body and machines with autonomy comparable to that of humans. Furthermore, a Taboo was established against the resurrection of a human being. Ultimately, all of these are considered unethical pursuits.

The Head has also set a number of restrictions on the creation and distribution of advanced firearms and on human cloning, which allow two copies of the same person to exist in the City for no longer than seven days.

It is also a City-wide Taboo to record a spell on digital media. A spell may be preserved only in a written book, such as a spellbook, or retained in the caster’s memory; no recording, file, device, or other digital storage can lawfully contain its formula. This prohibition applies whether the spell is copied, transmitted, photographed, scanned, or stored as data.

The Head is also responsible for various other Taboos of the City, such as not paying one's taxes, and violating a residential area during the Night in the Backstreets. The punishment for breaking any of these Taboos usually involve physical extermination, and for a Wing, the stripping of its qualifications and immediate shutdown.

The punishment for violating the Taboos established by the Head is noted to be different from breaking a Taboo of a Nest, which usually involves being chased by a small number of Taboo hunters; breaking a Taboo of the City is believed to be equivalent to a death sentence.

The Head is backed up in its functions by the Eye and the Claw, the two authorities responsible for B Corp. and C Corp. respectively. In enforcing the laws of the City, the three Wings make use of three types of agents: the Arbiters, the Beholders, and the Claws. When handling an Impurity-level threat, these agents are qualified to expel an entity from the City itself and move it into The Wastes.

Wings and Nests

The Wings of the World are a group of the twenty-six largest, most powerful conglomerates in the City. Each Wing acts as the governing body of a Nest of the City's District, and is commonly referred to with a letter from A to Z, corresponding to the number of the District in their control. Wings generally oversee all facets of living in the Nest of their District, while leaving the residents of the Backstreets to fend for themselves.

A Nest is the urban center of a District, which is under the direct management of its Wing. The citizens and employees of a Wing are commonly referred to as "feathers"; the status of a feather is widely considered one of the best and most secure paths in life, which the people of the Backstreets are often willing to go to great lengths to obtain. To gain access to a Nest, a corresponding Nest migration permit is required, while to be granted temporary passage one must be in possession of a visa unique to the specific Nest. Visas are implied to be relatively common, with corporate employees being provided with work visas that allow them as easy passage; Nest migration permits, on the other hand, are highly sought after, and implied to be expensive enough that even Grade 1 Fixers would struggle to obtain them. During the Smoke War, migration permits were offered as rewards to Fixers participating for K Corp. and I Corp., although by the end of the war the veterans were refused entry to the Nests.

Each Wing has its own cultural style and social structure which are reflected in how it governs its Nest, and the policies that regulate the way of life in the District's Backstreets. The laws of a Wing are called Taboos, and are enforced by specialized Fixers called Taboo hunters that police both the Nest and the Backstreets. While all Wings have Taboos, the enforcement and punishment for breaking them varies.

The Wings are directly ruled over by the Head, which bestows the status on a corporation that possesses and is able to successfully utilize a Singularity, a unique form of technology that is able to break the laws of physics.

As such, the Wings closely guard any information about their Singularity, employing military forces from their own ranks or from those of their business partners to avoid losing its secrets to rival Wings, industry competitors, or other forces. If a Wing's technology is stolen, fails commercially, or is viewed unfavorably by the Head, a Wing can collapse, resulting in the Nest losing its protection and being encroached by various factions that vie for its control, such as the Syndicates of the Backstreets. After a Wing collapses, a different corporation can attempt to ascend to the status of a Wing for that District.

Backstreets

The Backstreets are the areas of a District that lay outside the Wing's domain. The Backstreets are typically slums crowded with buildings, characterized by twisted and irregular roads, although there can be great differences between Districts.

Wings have differing degrees of presence, influence, and benevolence with regards to their Backstreets, although their relationship is ultimately exploitative. Citizens of the Backstreets are motivated to move up the ladder and into the Nests, while citizens of the Nests are motivated to maintain their statuses and preserve a more comfortable life under their Wing's protection. While not as secure as the Nest, residential areas are the safest parts of the Backstreets.

Given how strongly Backstreets denizens wish for a life in the Nest, Wings tend to uphold strict divides between the sectors, including security checkpoints and physical divisions. Security of borders between Nests and Backstreets vary from plasma walls to mere chain fences, but the Wings' power is such that no one in the Backstreets dares to cross over into a Nest without express permission.

As the Backstreets are not under the control and protection of the Wings, and even the Head's power struggles to reach within them, various factions operate freely in their place. The Backstreets are home to the headquarters of both Fixers, a type of mercenary for hire, who typically operate their Offices in the Backstreets, and organizations and gangs called Syndicates. Among Syndicates, the most important are the Five Fingers, the five largest Syndicates of the City which most other Syndicates answer to, and whose power is said to rival that of the Wings.

The Night in the Backstreets is another defining component of Backstreets life, as an 80 minute phenomena taking place from 3:14 to 4:34 AM. During this time period, nearly everything is allowed, as no one is held responsible for their deeds done during this time period. It is considered a brazenly savage, brutal, and vulgar period, even for the City. Beings known as Sweepers routinely become active during the Night in the Backstreets, and sweep through the streets in waves in order to consume any objects and structures located outside of permitted residential buildings, erasing all evidence of crime.

The Night in the Backstreets are regulated by only two Taboos: that residential areas mustn't be destroyed or violated in any way, including forcing the doors open, and that nothing which occurs during the Night in the Backstreets is recorded. The only form of testimony allowed during the Night in the Backstreets are direct eyewitnesses; even then, it is a Taboo mandated by the Head that no activity during this period are to be reported on. While the rules of the Night in the Backstreet don't apply to the Nest, it's viable to drag a Nest citizen into the Backstreets and dispose of them in this manner.

Culture

Magic is another part of the City’s shared vocabulary. A spell is a deliberate, limited shaping of the raw magic that suffuses the world, and its use is taught, inherited, researched, bought, or carried through Relics. Cantrips are practiced often enough to be cast at will, while more demanding spells require preparation or a spell fixed in the caster’s mind. A spellbook, a focus, a component pouch, and the correct material components can be as ordinary to a trained worker as a set of tools, though never equally affordable in every District.

The details of casting matter in public life. Verbal components make some magic impossible to hide; somatic components require a free hand; expensive material components can turn a useful spell into a luxury. Rituals offer a slower, less costly alternative when time permits. A tenant may use Alarm to protect a room, a researcher may use Identify to study an object, and a courier may depend on Sending when ordinary communication is unreliable.

The City’s people use the languages listed in the Standard and Exotic Languages rules, alongside regional languages and dialects. Some languages are families with many dialects, while others survive only in old records. Sweepers also speak a secret language of their own.

Various levels of education exist in the City. Schools exist both in the Nests and in the Backstreets, although other options are available, such as boarding schools and virtual schooling, both of which are considered safer options, as it allows the students to not have to cross dangerous alleyways on their way to school. In the Nest, homeschooling is shown to be an option as well.

The currency of the City is called dollars. It appears to be a common currency across the City, though with some exceptions, such as District 20, in which citizens commonly use their time, regulated through T Corp.'s Singularity, as currency. Money is extremely important to the people of the City, to the point it is a common belief that it can be used to solve any problem. The City is also characterized by great income gaps, both between the people of the Backstreets and the Nest, and among the people of the Nest itself, where only the higher classes can live comfortably, while the majority of the citizens use most of their income in living expenses and taxes, and don't have the ability to save up for emergencies.

Clothing styles show great variation between the four quarters of the City.

The fashion of the south is mostly inspired by real-life 21st century, western formal aesthetics, though with exceptions such as T Corp., where the fashion is instead inspired by the 19th century steampunk aesthetic the District is based on. The fashion of the east is instead based on real-life eastern, particularly Chinese fashion, ranging from traditional clothing to more modern interpretations. The clothing of the east, like that of the north and south, is mentioned to be mostly cloth-based. Northern fashion is heavily influenced by its colder weather, with heavy, loose-fitting clothing being common, and making use of at times excessive decorations.

The fashion of the west makes use of leather over cloth, and is noted to often include metal armor in its trends. Western fashion appears to be inspired by the real-life Middle Ages in Europe, borrowing elements from medieval armor.

City People

Spellcasting does not erase the City’s inequality. A cantrip may be available to someone who has practiced it, but a higher-level spell consumes spell points and a caster can only recover that resource through rest. Healing magic, protective wards, divinations, and mobility spells can change the outcome of a bad day, yet the training, time, components, and safe place to rest remain easier to secure in a Nest than in the Backstreets. A Relic that grants a spell can be a lifeline, an inheritance, or a debt that binds its bearer to an employer or Syndicate.

Everyday life in the City is defined by a constant struggle to stay afloat. While the people of The Wastes dream of living in the City, which they see as a safe place from the monsters that plague it, the people of the Backstreets strive to gain the wealth and power to gain access to a Nest, partially due to the influence of the Wings publicizing Nest dwellers' lives as the height of prosperity and comfort, and the people of the Nest struggle daily to keep their place inside it, while being constantly exploited by the Wings.

Regardless of their station, the people of the City are never free, and in order to survive, they typically tie themselves to various forms of organizations, seeking security and belonging in something larger than them. In doing so, they're willing to exploit others beneath them and make use of all the City can offer them, without caring about anyone else's well-being, and often taking pleasure in their suffering.

The people of the Backstreets must typically tie themselves to one of the Five Fingers or their subsidiaries to receive the necessary protection to carry on with their lives, while the people of the Nests depend on the Wings to shelter them. Because of this, Nest dwellers are colloquially known as "feathers". While the high level of security the Nest provides makes it the most ideal place to reside, its daily routine, which is often accompanied by overtime work and the constant fear of losing one's job and place in the Nest, can be stifling and oppressive to many of its citizens, with some recalling becoming listless as if they "lost focus, spacing out and staring into empty places".

The necessity for a connection to a group also means that the people of the City try not to form strong connections between individuals, as personal bonds can become a hindrance in one's abilities in the case of a loved one's death, which proves to be especially dangerous for those involved in Fixer work. The Cityfolk are still generally accustomed to such events, and it is not considered uncommon for them to swiftly move on by searching for a substitute.

In general, death, including mass casualties, is a common occurrence in the City. People all over the City are greatly accustomed to this, to the point even cases of 80,000 casualties are considered, while distressing, not particularly shocking, and not important enough to require special attention unless they happen to be within a Nest. Due to the frequency of such events, Cityfolk must learn to hide their grief and deal with it quickly, reflecting on the practice of funerals being uncommon in the City. Backstreets dweller instead typically let the bodies of the deceased be taken away during the Night in the Backstreets, to avoid the grave be desecrated by robbers looking to steal valuables of prosthetics from the body.

Furthermore, the availability of technologies such as prosthetic substitution, the ability to quickly reconstruct flesh, and Life Insurance in many ways trivialize pain and death itself for those who can afford it, leaving their users insensitive to such dangers.

These factors have led to the people of the City becoming apathetic and uncaring of other people's suffering, living without allowing themselves to truly feel their emotions and wishes, and lacking the curiosity to learn about the world they inhabit and the technologies they make use of every day, instead preferring to shut out reality altogether whenever possible. The L Corp. researcher Alice would come to describe these traits as a "disease of the mind", that plagued the people of the City, and that if left alone, it would bring them all to destruction. Lobotomy Corporation's work began as an attempt to cure this disease, and the Distortion phenomenon would be born in its aftermath.

The Cityfolk's feelings manifest in different ways as well. Their desires are mentioned to beget concepts comparable to "gods", such as the Prescripts of the Index, which are generated from the people's wish for a direction in life. This same desire is what often brings the Cityfolk to join cults that promise to grant them a purpose, such as the Church of Gears. Beyond these forms, however, the City currently lacks major forms of organized religion, although it is suggested that they have existed in the past.

Fixers & Association

In the City, a common path in life for those without better options is that of the Fixer. A Fixer is a sort of handyman, who can be hired for everything from menial tasks to active combat. Fixers are subject to a grading system based on the missions they complete. A Fixer starts at Grade 9 and can be promoted to a higher grade, all the way up to 1; in the case of a Fixer being caught breaking work regulations, they can lose their status and be demoted to a lower grade.

A majority of Fixers congregate in Fixer Offices, small to mid-sized businesses led by and composed of Fixers. Offices process requests dedicated to whomever in their ranks may be best suited. Offices can work independently, or they can be associated with particular Associations of Fixers, which are larger professional organizations with City-wide influence, with particular interests aimed at the Fixers working beneath them.

There are twelve Associations in the City, each of which manages certain types of requests common to Fixer work, such as the Zwei Association focusing on protection missions, or the Devyat' Association focusing on delivery across the City. Associations control and distribute Fixer tasks, and can take jobs from anywhere within the City, as they have individual branches set up in various Districts. Branches are divided into four groups corresponding to the cardinal directions of the City, and employees are further divided by grade into sections ranging from 1 to 6, with lower number sections corresponding to higher Fixer grades.

Every Association has its own rules, specialties, uniform, and agreements with other factions. The Associations are governed by the Hana Association, which oversees everything related to the Fixer system, including Fixer grades.

In the case of a Grade 1 Fixer demonstrating great renown in any particular field of work, they may be considered beyond the grading system, and receive the title of Color from Hana Association as a way to signify their exceptional skill. Color Fixers can keep working in their Offices, or be contracted by a company. They are admired throughout the City, and it is a common belief that the life of a Color is one of true freedom. Despite this, Color Fixers are still bound to the regulations of Hana Associations and of the Head, and due to their ability they're often made to handle the most dangerous hazards of the City.

Fixer work gives spellcasting a practical place alongside weapons, augments, and contracts. An Office may take a protection request that calls for Alarm, Mage Armor, or Protection from Evil and Good; an investigation may require Detect Magic, Identify, or Zone of Truth; and a pursuit can turn on Misty Step, Darkness, or Dispel Magic. These are tools rather than guarantees. A caster still needs the range, components, time, and concentration stated by the spell, and a Fixer in armor must be proficient with it to cast at all.

Syndicates

Syndicates are a type of organization in the City, and can be seen as the unregulated counterpart to Fixer Offices, being comparable to gangs with various goals and methods of operating. In general, the term Syndicate is used to refer to organized groups that aren't tied to the responsibilities and restrictions that come with being a Fixer.

Some Syndicates focus on extorting money from people for protection, while others abduct people, kill for fun and pleasure, or do anything in between. Syndicates mostly operate in the Backstreets, but are not restricted to them. They can be small groups that only exist in one corner of the Backstreets, or large enough to exist in every District in the City.

Syndicates can be hired to do contract jobs, although there is higher risk compared to contracting an Office or Association, as they are not bound to the same regulations Fixers are. Sometimes, Wings will hire Syndicate members for criminal projects that cannot be handled officially.

Syndicates tend to have a culture unique to their group, and a way of living that distinguishes them from other Syndicates. Many Syndicates have a culture focused around replicating family dynamics and consider each other family, with some even referring to each other with familial terms. This can result in Syndicates with a strict focus on respect for those who are higher ranked, or in Syndicates whose members care deeply for each other and protect one another at any cost.

Syndicates are graded through the City's hazard system, measuring their danger level and notability. Syndicates can grow under these aspects and gain power in many ways, with one of them being the practice of raiding and wiping out Fixers' Offices. The larger and more experienced an Office is, the greater the notoriety that is achieved by defeating it.

Above all Syndicates are the Five Fingers, or "the hand of the Backstreets", five Star of the City-level Syndicates, whose power rivals that of the Wings. Each of the Five Fingers has a distinct culture, and demands different things from people seeking protection under them. Other Syndicates can become subsidiaries of the Five Fingers, granting them powerful connections as long as they abide by the Finger's orders.

Technology

Denizens of the City have developed a wide variety of fantastical technologies that they use on a daily basis.

People from all quarters of the City make ample use of body modification procedures, which allow individuals to augment their strength or replace lost body parts. Prosthetic substitution involves the replacement of organic parts with mechanical or bionic ones, making it possible for a person to replace their entire body, with the exception of the brain. Due to the Head's Taboo against copies of human bodies, this makes the sight of an individual with mechanical limbs or a display in place of their head incredibly common in the City.

Furthermore, City denizens can make use of various types of life insurance policy, such as the one provided by H Corp., that allow resuscitation after death. Thanks to these policies, individuals who can afford the cost can be brought back to life in a new body without violating the Head's Ethics Amendment, as long as their brain remained intact.

Augmentation procedures are also used by a large demographic of people, from Syndicate members to wealthy Nest citizens. Augmentation is particularly common among those involved in Fixer business, to the point it's considered virtually impossible to do Fixer work without investing in augmentation procedures. Through these procedures, an individual might obtain superhuman speed or strength, or strengthen their body parts to become impervious to various types of attacks.

The technological innovation of the City also reflects on its healthcare. Through the research of the Wings and the power of their Singularities, such of K Corp.'s and H Corp.'s, nearly every health problem has become curable on a theoretical level. In practice, only those who can afford it are able to pay to make use of these technologies.

This goes for most of the innovation of the City. In most cases, these technologies are available at various levels of quality, with the most expensive and safe being used by Nest citizens, while the people of the Backstreets can make use of cheaper versions that can cause unwanted side effects, as is the case for memory wipe procedures.

Magic and technology are intertwined without being the same thing. A spell produces a discrete effect when a caster shapes magic; a device, prosthetic, or Singularity is a constructed system that may sustain an effect, package it as a service, or exploit a principle no ordinary spell can reproduce. A clinic might use surgery, an H Corp. policy, and spells such as Cure Wounds or Lesser Restoration in the same treatment, while still reserving its safest care for those who can pay.

Despite the level of innovation of the City generally being incredibly high, its access isn't uniform across the various Districts. While cell phones, the Internet, and various public transport systems exist, they are not universally available. For example, advanced communication systems aren't available in T Corp.'s District, with its citizens making use of letters instead.

Singularities

A Singularity should not be confused with a spell, even when both create an effect that looks miraculous. A spell is a bounded expression of magic with a casting time, range, components, duration, and a caster’s limits. A Singularity is a proprietary source of industrial power whose effects can reorganize a District’s transport, medicine, labor, warfare, or environment. The distinction is why a Wing protects a Singularity as a state secret while ordinary spellcasting remains part of the City’s daily life.

Singularities are the technologies that define the Wings. They are not merely advanced inventions: each is an impossible principle, object, process, or system that can violate the ordinary limits of the world. A corporation that can possess, exploit, and protect a Singularity gains the military strength and revenue required to seek Wing status; a Wing that loses control of its Singularity risks collapse.

The modern order of Wings began when a traveler from The Ruins brought Z Corp. an object infused with the power of The Wastes. Its discovery changed the corporate arms race. Weapons and spells alone had become too costly to distinguish one corporation from another, but a Singularity could create an entire industry, make a District indispensable, and give its owner a form of power no competitor could reproduce reliably.

No two Singularities need resemble one another. Their known effects range from restoration and preservation to the manipulation of gravity, time, dreams, containment, locking, erasure, virtual experience, and space. They may appear as a manufactured device, a natural or recovered object, a procedure, an environment, or a service. The product sold to the public is often only a controlled application of the underlying phenomenon.

District life is shaped by the Singularity of its Wing. G Corp. uses gravity manipulation for artificial wings, levitating vehicles, and its floating Nest. T Corp. turns time and light into resources. W Corp.’s Singularity harnesses Warp, the radiation-like energy that emanates from The Ruins and The Wastes, to promise travel between distant parts of the City in seconds. K Corp. and H Corp. turn their medical technologies into healing, agriculture, bodily alteration, and weapons. Each benefit has a price, a patent, and an owner.

A Singularity is also a secret worth killing for. Wings compartmentalize their research, employ corporate forces and contracted Fixers, erase records, and conduct wars to keep competitors from learning how it works. Imitations and stolen derivatives circulate through the City, but they are unreliable, dangerous, or illegal precisely because they lack the original owner’s control. A Singularity can be bought around, copied imperfectly, or fought over, but never treated as an ordinary commodity.

The Ruins remain a source of both relics and unknown Singularities. Delvers, Fixer expeditions, corporate prospectors, and scavengers descend in search of them. Their discoveries can raise a corporation into a Wing, destabilize a District, or create a new hazard before anyone understands what was found.

Hazards

Magic changes how hazards are investigated and answered, not whether they are dangerous. A Fixer can search for a lingering magical effect, counter a spell with Dispel Magic, shelter a group behind an abjuration, or use divination to find a missing person. None of that guarantees safety against an abnormality, a Distortion, a hostile caster, or a failing Singularity. Area spells can also turn a crowded Backstreets fight into a larger disaster, which is why responsible Offices treat a caster’s range, target, and area of effect as part of the job’s risk.

A hazard is any threat that endangers people, a District, a Wing’s interests, or the City’s imposed order. Hazards are not limited to monsters. A hostile Syndicate, a failed Singularity, a Distortion, a contaminated excavation site, a corporate military operation, an escaped abnormality, or a public disaster can all be treated as hazards depending on the damage they cause and the attention they attract.

The City grades hazards by danger and notability. This makes the classification political as well as practical: an event that destroys Backstreets lives may be ignored, contained, or quietly exploited, while the same threat inside a Nest can bring immediate corporate action. Fixer Offices and Associations take paid requests against lower-scale threats; Wings deploy their own forces when the threat affects a District’s profits, image, or Singularity.

Some dangers are recognized as Stars of the City. A Color Fixer, the Five Fingers, a powerful Syndicate, or another extraordinary force can reach that level of notoriety. A Star is not automatically an enemy. It is a force whose ability and influence are too great for the City to dismiss, and whose continued existence may be tolerated so long as it does not violate The Head’s vision.

The highest designation is Impuritas Civitatis, an Impurity of the City. The Head assigns this classification only to something it considers fundamentally incompatible with the City’s ethics. A City-wide Taboo, an autonomous artificial creature, an unlawful human copy, or a threat that overturns the established order can be judged an Impurity. The purpose of the designation is not negotiation: the subject must be destroyed or expelled through The Ruins into The Wastes.

Responses depend on the scale of the threat. Taboo hunters enforce a Wing’s local laws. Offices, Associations, and contracted Syndicates deal with work that can be paid for or denied publicly. The Eye, the Claw, Arbiters, Beholders, and Claws act when The Head intervenes. This hierarchy ensures that many hazards are handled as business before they are recognized as disasters.

Monsters

Monsters are part of Elysium’s environment, not an anomaly outside it. Some are creatures surviving in The Wastes amid godblood, Warp, and distorted mana. Others emerge from The Ruins, where dead gods, buried kingdoms, lost technologies, relics, and damaged reality lie beneath the City. Expeditions into those places are valuable because of what they can recover, but they are never safe.

District Z demonstrates the City’s relationship with monsters most clearly. Its excavation and dredging work supplies Elysium with durable metals, relics, and possible Singularities. Old sites and collapsed tunnels are often left behind once they stop being profitable, becoming hellholes inhabited by Warp-touched life. The Wing does not make extermination its first priority; people who enter to scavenge are expected to accept the risk.

Some creatures are the results of corporate use rather than wilderness. The Smoke War left insect-like prosthetic survivors and refugees in its wake. H Corp.’s biological products can alter bodies and create animal-like combatants. The Great Lake of District U contains enormous Whales and distinct biomes governed by their own conditions. A monster may be a natural survivor, a changed person, a manufactured weapon, or the unintended consequence of a Wing’s technology.

Gift Gnomes and other peoples of the City should not be confused with monsters simply because they are unusual. The Head’s historic age of humanity and its Taboos concern status, autonomy, and control; corporate propaganda often blurs those distinctions when it serves a Wing’s interests. In practice, the City’s institutions decide what is called a creature, a citizen, a product, or a hazard.

Monsters are also distinct from abnormalities and Distortions. Abnormalities began appearing after the White Nights and Dark Days from the devastation surrounding L Corp.’s branches. Distortions arise when a person’s emotion, desire, and despair overwhelm their human form. Both may be treated as monsters by witnesses and corporations, but their origins are tied to the City’s history and the unresolved legacy of L Corp., E.G.O, and Alice’s research.

Sweepers

Sweepers are creatures that become active during the Night in the Backstreets, the eighty minutes from 3:14 to 4:34 AM. They travel through the Backstreets in waves and consume what is left outside permitted residential buildings: bodies, possessions, structures, evidence, and the remains of violence. For residents, the warning to get indoors before the Night is not custom. It is survival.

Their work gives the Night its particular cruelty. Nearly anything can happen during those eighty minutes, yet the Sweepers remove the physical evidence before the morning. This does not make the Night lawless. Two City-wide Taboos remain absolute: a residential area cannot be destroyed or violated, including by forcing open a door, and nothing that occurs during the Night may be recorded or reported.

Only direct eyewitness testimony is permitted, and even that cannot become a report of the Night’s events. The prohibition protects the City’s silence as much as it regulates the creatures. A person may be dragged from a Nest into the Backstreets and disposed of during the Night, while the Nest itself remains outside the Night’s ordinary rules.

Sweepers speak a secret language. It is separate from the Standard and Exotic Languages used by City people, from regional dialects, and from the terminology of the Associations. Knowledge that the language exists does not grant an ordinary speaker the ability to understand it; it remains a guarded and alien part of Backstreets life.

Backstreets residents build their routines around the Night. Bodies are often left for the Sweepers rather than buried, both because death is common and because graves invite robbers seeking valuable prosthetics. The result is a City where disappearance, cleanup, and silence are treated as normal conditions of life.

History

The City’s history begins with the collapse of the Old World. Before gods and magic became part of mortal life, humanity possessed weapons and technology capable of reaching beyond its world. Contact with the gods of other worlds brought curiosity, greed, jealousy, and eventually war. Human nations divided around divine powers, and the old order gave way to an empire shaped by faith, arcana, and the occult.

The Empire was not simply a human nation. It was a tool through which pantheons exercised influence over the world. Mortals who resisted were overwhelmed by powers their former defenses could not match. The brief peace after the Empire’s victory failed because the gods themselves lacked a common purpose. Their followers turned upon one another in the Empyrean War.

The Empyrean War joined divine magic to the world-ending weapons that the Old World had refused to use. Gods and mortals built devices capable of poisoning reality itself. Cornered powers chose mutual destruction rather than submission. When the war ended, the world below was molten and scarred by Warp, dead gods, godblood, and distorted mana: the place now known as The Wastes.

That war also explains why magic in Elysium is treated with both familiarity and caution. The same art can provide a ward, repair a wound, reveal a hidden truth, or destroy a street. The City’s spellcasters work within rules of preparation, components, range, and finite spell points, but the memory of magic combined with unrestricted technology remains embedded in corporate policy, military research, and The Head’s fear of anything it cannot contain.

Surviving peoples were evacuated into demiplanes, but those shelters could only support them temporarily. As resources dwindled and faith weakened, the gods and mortals needed a permanent refuge. The Ruins, a protected catacomb for dead mortals, rulers, dragons, and gods, had survived the wars as a memorial. The decision to build on it was contested as desecration, but the alternative was to leave the living without a home.

Laborers and craftsmen raised Elysium above The Ruins. Space demanded vertical construction, and the City grew into a layered megastructure rather than spreading across the poisoned world. Most gods were gone, dead, weakened, or left behind as the survivors of many peoples became concentrated within one structure.

In AE 56, segregation and conflict allowed arms profiteers to turn fear into wealth and political power. Regulations and social classes were presented as protection, while separating the influential from the poor. In AE 74, this new ruling order named itself The Head. It divided the City into Districts, placed A, B, and C at the peak, and formalized the divide between Nests and Backstreets.

The Head allowed major corporations to govern individual Districts because it could not manage every layer directly. Their rivalry became the War of the Wings. In AE 184, Z Corp. received an object from a traveler out of The Ruins: a Singularity. By AE 185, the ability to harness a Singularity became central to a corporation’s claim to Wing status. The Smoke War, the fall of L Corp., the White Nights and Dark Days, and the emergence of abnormalities, Distortions, and E.G.O all followed within the City’s later history.

The Old World

The Old World was when gods and magic didn't exist, it was an age of humans where conflicts arose from interest and aggression and all was standing at the edge of destruction with weapons no mortal should be capable of reaching. They were capable of reaching for the other planets within the solar system, sending messages out into the void of space where a single line would reach the gods above, introducing them to a canvas. Unfortunately for humans, the deities of the other worlds were not unlike humans, their curiosity turned into greed, jealousy, envy amongst one another. Not before long, war broke out, and the long standing treaty between nations fell from the words of those that picked the side of gods.

The Empire

The Empire was not a nation built by humans, but a nation that was a tool that gods of a pantheon could rally behind to enact their influence over the world. Humans that attempted to resist the words of the gods bent and eventually broke from their power, their world and defenses useless to the magics of faith, arcana, and the occult that have been built up over centuries in other worlds. What the humans built up in their whole history simple crumbled, many chose to hide or cease their being rather than to watch as the deities built over The Old World.

The Empyrean War

The Empyrean War was an inevitable event that were caused by the gods lacking a common goal, to rid the world of the faithless and to rule over it. The silence after The Empire's victory against the humans of The Old World was a short lived time of peace, before the gods began to aim their followers against one another. This was not like any war of the gods that occured in the other worlds, this was a war that included the world ending weapons that the humans of The Old World had chosen not to use for the elongevity of mankind, even when most integrated into the races that followed the gods. The gods used magic and technology, combining them to make planar destroying devices that could rend realities and make it poison to even deities. Gods were to alike to the mortals, cloaked in rage, drowning in sorrow, but even more susceptible to pride. Those that were cornered chose to sacrifice the world if need be, even if there were agreements in place, no god would bend the knee to another if they still had an opportunity, even if that opportunity was mutually assured destruction.

The Fallout

The Fallout of The Empyrean War was evacuating each living race to demiplanes whilst the world became a molten rock of distorted mana and dead gods. Not even the deities could recover from the weapons they used on each other, and it became The Wastes: a poisoned expanse of godblood, Warp, and mana. The demiplanes could only shoulder the mortals for temporary amount of time before the resources inside dwindled. The faith of the mortals slowly lost and the gods became weak from their follower's distrust and boiling hatred. The next step was clear, making a garden, a home, a city for the mortals, shielded from The Wastes that the gods created. It was an act some deities did out of responsibility, most due to their lack of ability to shelter their followers with their powers.

The Ruins

The Ruins was a catacomb for all sorts of dead mortals, kings, pharaohs, dragons, and sometimes, gods. It was a sacred place that stood tall amongst the rubble due to a concentrated effort to shield it from the wars by deities and followers who saw it as an important memorial of those that were lost. The deities built atop of it, there were gods that were against it, calling it desecration, but they could not voice their opinions for they were the minority, and the majority rather chose to continue with the project than soon be part of The Ruins.

The City of Elysium

The city was built through the shepherding of carpenters and laborers ontop of the ruins, there they began to build a city. Where space demanded, they constructed floors and layers for the city. They could not build wide due to being only on one structure, but it was a mega-structure that could very well hold the weight of the world. At the end, only a few gods chose to remain within the world and some were killed due to dwindling followers. The era of faith had come and gone with the last surviving mortals of the world now concentrated in a single structure.

AE 56Segregation

Without one banner to stand under, races flocked to familiarities, families, culture. They stood with their own race and species, putting them first than others, leading to infighting with the mortals. In the fog of war, there were those that sought profit, and profit did come. Weapons were the first reinventions from the war against the humans of The Old World, the profiteers fed into the fear and anxiety of people and armed them with blades and guns. The profiteers only made themselves known as the smoke settled, creating a force under them that could do their bidding, creating regulations, rules, and class amongst the mortals. It was first hidden as a law to help prevent the unfair treatment against races, but it was a ploy to seperate the wealthy, influential, and the poor. The era of wealth had risen, and without the gods' power to snuff out the mistreatment and immoral actions, the mortals named themselves the leaders of the city. That was when The Head was created.

AE 74The Head

The Head was a government body that put itself on top after profiteering off of conflicts between mortals, they were the first manufacturers of weapons and the creator of the city's laws. Those that didn't bend the knee would be cast out to The Ruins, and even thrown out straight into The Wastes below. The mortals were forced into a line that had strict rules, any attempts to overthrow the system were stomped and straightened, technology and magic blended together to create an unstoppable, ruthless rule with one goal in mind: to extend the longevity of mortal-kind by whatever means necessary and remain the top power of the city. The layers were divided into Districts, and each District was split between Nests and Backstreets. Nests were the common living places for the middle and upper classes, while Backstreets were the slums where the poor and outcasts were left to survive. The Head placed itself at the top of the city in District A, with Districts B and C underneath it, and so on. The price of living in each District was naturally based on its verticality: the higher Districts were farther from The Ruins and Warp in The Wastes, while District Z stood one fall away from The Ruins and endured daily Warp exposure. Then came the corporations. The city had large numbers of corporations, but The Head couldn't manage each District at the same time, so it allowed large enough corporations to gain the ability to create and enforce laws within the specific Districts they governed. This created conflict that would become known as the War of the Wings.

AE 184The Wings

The Wings are large corporations able to create and enforce laws within the Districts they govern. A Wing can be overthrown if its revenue and shares no longer meet the threshold needed to sustain its District, or if its owner and board members are killed by rival corporations. As conflicts progressed, each corporation that became a Wing found it difficult to maintain its status through an arms race of weapons and spells that no one could keep up with. This changed when a traveler from The Ruins came to Z Corp., the Wing governing District Z, and showed them an item imbued with the power of The Wastes: a singularity.

AE 185Singularities

Singularities are a concept of tremendous power, those corporations that sought to be wings and maintain their status needs to have a singularity to differentiate themselves from the rest as harnessing a singularity will bring in massive amounts of profits and military power. Singularities are well kept secrets, from objects that cry tears of rejuvination to be used as serums to heal farm animals from being slaughtered, to the preservation of bodies through a 20,000 year voyage into another dimension. Fixers were the errand boys of large corporations or people in wealth and/or in power, pay a fixer office or association to get a job done without having the guilt or shame of commiting it. Some fixers specialized in locating singularities specifically, being delvers of the ruins. With the singularities came imitations, or technology closely resembling their power or technology that could even rival their power, these technologies were often stolen for the benefit of the wings. A name for mercenaries became prevalent, often being called to fix a situation.

Now the corporations that made them had an actual edge if they had an invention patented which bred competetiveness between each corporation and each wing, but each patent required that corporation to become unable to refuse service to The Head if they required their inventions. This didn't extend to singularities themselves, but it kept a leash on each wing and made The Head stronger as each corporation invented more technology.
