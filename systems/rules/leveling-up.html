SYSTEMS: RULES: Leveling Up

As your character takes part in the story and the plot progresses, he or she gains experience, represented by experience points. Experience is awarded for meaningful story developments, discoveries, decisions, and milestones rather than for slaying enemies or overcoming individual challenges.

Your character can have between 0 and 10,000 experience points. The GM awards experience when the plot reaches a significant milestone, and the amount awarded depends on how important that milestone is. Experience points can’t exceed 10,000. When your character reaches 10,000 experience points, your character gains a level and their experience points reset to 0.

When your character gains a level, his or her class often grants additional features, as detailed in the class description. Some of these features allow you to increase your ability scores. You can’t increase an ability score above 20. In addition, every character’s proficiency bonus increases at certain levels.

Each time you gain a level, you gain 1 additional Hit Die. Roll that Hit Die, add your Constitution modifier to the roll, and add the total to your hit point maximum. Alternatively, you can use the fixed value shown in your class entry, which is the average result of the die roll (rounded up).

When your Constitution modifier increases by 1, your hit point maximum increases by 1 for each level you have attained. For example, if your 7th-level fighter has a Constitution score of 18, when he reaches 8th level, he increases his Constitution score from 17 to 18, thus increasing his Constitution modifier from +3 to +4. His hit point maximum then increases by 8.
