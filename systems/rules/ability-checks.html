SYSTEMS: RULES: Ability Checks

Ability Checks

An ability check tests a character's or monster's innate talent and training in an effort to overcome a challenge. The GM calls for an ability check when a character or monster attempts an action (other than an attack) that has a chance of failure. When the outcome is uncertain, the die determines the results.

For every ability check, the GM decides which ability is relevant to the task and the task's Difficulty Class (DC).

To make an ability check, roll a d20 and add the relevant ability modifier, applying bonuses and penalties. If the total equals or exceeds the DC, the check succeeds; otherwise, it fails.

Contests

Sometimes one character's or monster's efforts are directly opposed to another's. Both participants make ability checks appropriate to their efforts, and the higher total wins. In a tie, the situation remains as it was before the contest.

Skills

Each ability covers a broad range of capabilities, including skills that a character or monster can be proficient in. A skill represents a specific aspect of an ability score, and proficiency demonstrates focused training in that aspect.

The GM can call for an ability check using a specific skill. If the character is proficient in that skill, the proficiency bonus is added to the check; otherwise, the character makes a normal ability check.

Skills with Different Abilities

Normally, proficiency in a skill applies only to a specific kind of ability check. Proficiency in Athletics, for example, usually applies to Strength checks. In some situations, though, your proficiency might reasonably apply to a different kind of check. In such cases, the GM might ask for a check using an unusual combination of ability and skill, or you might ask the GM if you can apply a proficiency to a different check. For example, if you have to swim from an offshore island to the mainland, your GM might call for a Constitution check to see if you have the stamina to make it that far. In this case, the GM might allow you to apply your proficiency in Athletics and ask for a Constitution (Athletics) check. Similarly, when your half-orc barbarian uses a display of raw strength to intimidate an enemy, your GM might ask for a Strength (Intimidation) check, even though Intimidation is normally associated with Charisma. If the application is especially far-fetched, you may offer to discard an Inspiration or Drama Card to be allowed to roll with that proficiency.

Passive Checks

A passive check is a special kind of ability check that doesn't involve a die roll. It can represent the average result of a task done repeatedly or help the GM determine whether characters notice something without rolling.

A passive check total equals 10 + all modifiers that normally apply to the check. Add 5 for advantage and subtract 5 for disadvantage.

Working Together

Sometimes two or more characters team up to attempt a task. The character leading the effort can make the ability check with advantage, reflecting the help provided by the others. In combat, this requires the Help action.

A character can provide help only if they are proficient in the task being attempted, or if they have an applicable proficiency from Skills with Different Abilities and can reasonably argue that it applies to the task. The helper must also be able to attempt the task alone. The GM can decide that a task cannot be made more productive by additional help.

Group Checks

When a number of individuals are trying to accomplish something as a group, the GM might ask for a group ability check. Everyone in the group makes the check; if at least half the group succeeds, the whole group succeeds.

Critical Rolls

A roll of 20 on the d20, or succeeding by 10 or more, is a Critical Success. A roll of 1, or failing by 10 or more, is a Critical Fumble. Apply the following degree of success to the result of the check.

Critical Success. Good fortune creates a positive outcome beyond normal success or lessens the consequences of a bad outcome.

Success. The character succeeds at the task normally.

Failure. The character fails the task and suffers its normal consequences.

Critical Fumble. Bad fortune turns a bad outcome into something worse or adds a new complication determined by the GM.

During a group check, a Critical Success counts as two successes, while a Critical Fumble counts as two failures.
