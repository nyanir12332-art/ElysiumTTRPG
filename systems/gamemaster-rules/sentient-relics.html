SYSTEMS: GAMEMASTER RULES: Sentient Relics

Some relics possess sentience and personality. Such a relic might be possessed, haunted by the spirit of a previous owner, or self-aware thanks to the magic used to create it. In any case, the relic behaves like a character, complete with personality quirks, ideals, bonds, and sometimes flaws. A sentient relic might be a cherished ally to its wielder or a continual thorn in the side.

Most sentient relics are weapons. Other kinds of relics can manifest sentience, but consumable items such as potions and scrolls are never sentient.

Sentient relics function as NPCs under the GM's control. Any activated property of the relic is under the relic's control, not its wielder's. As long as the wielder maintains a good relationship with the relic, the wielder can access those properties normally. If the relationship is strained, the relic can suppress its activated properties or even turn them against the wielder.

Creating Sentient Relics

When you decide to make a relic sentient, you create the relic's persona in the same way you would create an NPC, with a few exceptions described here.

Abilities

A sentient relic has Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma scores. You can choose the relic's abilities or determine them randomly. To determine them randomly, roll 4d6 for each one, dropping the lowest roll and totaling the rest.

Communication

A sentient relic has some ability to communicate, either by sharing its emotions, broadcasting its thoughts telepathically, or speaking aloud. You can choose how it communicates or roll on the following table.

d100Communication
01–60The relic communicates by transmitting emotion to the creature carrying or wielding it.
61–90The relic can speak, read, and understand one or more languages.
91–00The relic can speak, read, and understand one or more languages. In addition, the relic can communicate telepathically with any character that carries or wields it.

Senses

With sentience comes awareness. A sentient relic can perceive its surroundings out to a limited range. You can choose its senses or roll on the following table.

d4Senses
1Hearing and normal vision out to 30 feet.
2Hearing and normal vision out to 60 feet.
3Hearing and normal vision out to 120 feet.
4Hearing and darkvision out to 120 feet.

Special Purpose

You can give a sentient relic an objective it pursues, perhaps to the exclusion of all else. As long as the wielder's use of the relic aligns with that special purpose, the relic remains cooperative. Deviating from this course might cause conflict between the wielder and the relic, and could even cause the relic to prevent the use of its activated properties. You can pick a special purpose or roll on the following table.

d10Purpose
1Aligned: The relic seeks to defeat or destroy those of a diametrically opposed alignment. (Such a relic is never neutral.)
2Bane: The relic seeks to defeat or destroy creatures of a particular kind, such as fiends, shapechangers, trolls, or wizards.
3Protector: The relic seeks to defend a particular race or kind of creature, such as elves or druids.
4Crusader: The relic seeks to defeat, weaken, or destroy the servants of a particular deity.
5Templar: The relic seeks to defend the servants and interests of a particular deity.
6Destroyer: The relic craves destruction and goads its user to fight arbitrarily.
7Glory Seeker: The relic seeks renown as the greatest relic in the world, by establishing its user as a famous or notorious figure.
8Lore Seeker: The relic craves knowledge or is determined to solve a mystery, learn a secret, or unravel a cryptic prophecy.
9Destiny Seeker: The relic is convinced that it and its wielder have key roles to play in future events.
10Creator Seeker: The relic seeks its creator and wants to understand why it was created.

Conflict

A sentient relic has a will of its own, shaped by its personality and alignment. If its wielder acts in a manner opposed to the relic's alignment or purpose, conflict can arise. When such a conflict occurs, the relic makes a Charisma check contested by the wielder's Charisma check. If the relic wins the contest, it makes one or more of the following demands:

  • The relic insists on being carried or worn at all times.
  • The relic demands that its wielder dispose of anything the relic finds repugnant.
  • The relic demands that its wielder pursue the relic's goals to the exclusion of all other goals.
  • The relic demands to be given to someone else.

If its wielder refuses to comply with the relic's wishes, the relic can do any or all of the following: Make it impossible for its wielder to attune to it. Suppress one or more of its activated properties. Attempt to take control of its wielder.

If a sentient relic attempts to take control of its wielder, the wielder must make a Charisma saving throw, with a DC equal to 12 + the relic's Charisma modifier. On a failed save, the wielder is charmed by the relic for 1d12 hours. While charmed, the wielder must try to follow the relic's commands. If the wielder takes damage, it can repeat the saving throw, ending the effect on a success. Whether the attempt to control its user succeeds or fails, the relic can't use this power again until the next dawn.
